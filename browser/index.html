What does each calculation tell us?

From a gate to a schedule

Start with X on q₀. The circuit asks us to exchange |0⟩ and |1⟩. In the ideal two-level model a resonant drive does this when its integrated rotation rate is π. The two components of the drive envelope, I and Q, set the rotation axis. Y rotates by the same angle about an axis 90° away. A virtual Z instead shifts the phase reference for later drives. Try the phase-test circuit to see HZH turn that phase change into a bit flip.

The schedule uses an idealized echoed ZX interaction to implement CX. Measurement adds a probe tone and an acquisition window for the returning signal. The JavaScript model computes the ideal circuit from |000⟩, with projective measurements and a record of the last outcome on each measured wire. The W-state circuit follows the original notebook. Its pulse templates and timings are illustrative.

Why can a pulse leave the qubit?

A transmon has energy levels above |1⟩. Holding the intended rotation fixed while shortening the pulse raises the drive strength. The drive can then populate higher levels. If pn(t) is the population of level n, the leakage at time t is 1 − p0(t) − p1(t). Compare how much population leaves the qubit during the drive and how much remains outside it at the end.

The leakage experiment takes one local drive from the editor and starts a separate transmon in |0⟩ by default. QuTiP evolves a rotating-frame Duffing oscillator, a model with unequally spaced energy levels. The result depends on the specified anharmonicity, pulse shape and level truncation. It describes an isolated pulse. Earlier gates, couplers, neighboring drives and readout resonators are outside this calculation.

What changes if we change the chip?

Suppose we move a drive electrode away from a transmon. To predict how that changes the pulse response, we need the electromagnetic coupling of the new geometry. EmulateQPU-4Q starts with a parameterized layout and extracts electrical quantities using Gmsh and Palace. Circuit quantization turns them into energy levels and interactions. Pulse calibration chooses the controls, and multilevel evolution shows how the resulting model responds across several transmons.

Independent solvers can check the numerical calculation. Predictions for a fabricated chip also need measured calibration, and the four-transmon reference documents open work on mounted-device RF convergence and readout linewidths.
